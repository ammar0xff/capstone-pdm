⚡ Dynamic RMS Vibration
ISO 20816
RMSdyn = √ [ 1⁄N ∑ (adyn[i])2 ]

💡 Engineering Reason:
Measures continuous destructive kinetic energy and mechanical vibration power according to ISO 20816 standards. It rises during gross mechanical deterioration such as rotor unbalance, shaft misalignment, or structural mounting looseness.
💥 Vibration Kurtosis (4th Moment)
Bearing Defects
κ = μ4⁄σ4 = 1⁄N ∑ (adyn - μ)4⁄[ 1⁄N ∑ (adyn - μ)2 ]2
κ = 0.0000017 / (0.00074)2 = 2.65 (Gaussian ∼ 3.0)

Measures probability density "tailedness". Healthy background vibration is Gaussian (κ ≈ 3.0). Microscopic spalls and cracks on bearing raceways generate sharp transient impact shocks that cause kurtosis to explode to 6.0–15.0 weeks before RMS shows any noticeable rise.
📈 Crest Factor (Impact Ratio)
Fault Stages
CF = Peakdyn⁄RMSdyn

💡 Engineering Reason:
Ratio of peak impact shock to baseline continuous vibration. In Stage 2 bearing wear (incipient flaking), peak spikes while RMS stays low, driving CF > 4.5–8.0. In terminal Stage 4 failure, general wear raises RMS and CF collapses back down.
