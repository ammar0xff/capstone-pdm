Industrial Problem & Motivation

The Real-World Crisis: Unplanned Factory Shutdowns

Electric induction motors drive over 70% of all mechanical power in industry. Their unexpected failure halts assembly lines and destroys profitability.

Reactive Maintenance
"Run it until it blows up." Destroys shafts, ruins production batches, creates factory fire hazards, and repair costs exceed planned maintenance by up to 500%.
Preventive Maintenance
"Replace parts every 90 days on a calendar." Highly wasteful: studies show up to 80% of replaced bearings were completely healthy. Technicians also induce assembly faults during unnecessary teardowns.
Our Predictive Paradigm
"Replace only when physical degradation is mathematically confirmed." Continuous multi-modal condition monitoring flags microscopic raceway spalling and mass imbalance weeks before catastrophic seizure.

Why Edge Computing over Pure Cloud?

Raw 100 Hz vibration sampling generates millions of records per hour. Streaming raw high-frequency waveforms to cloud APIs causes severe latency, costs thousands in egress fees, and fails when the internet drops. Edge DSP computes features locally (inference benchmarked at 30.0 ms on the Raspberry Pi 4).

The 0-EGP Cloud Philosophy

We engineered our telemetry around Google Firebase's generous Free Spark Tier. Non-blocking JSON streaming maintains sub-second synchronization worldwide without paying a single piastre in server fees.

Edge Artificial Intelligence

Two-Stage Coarse-to-Fine Inference Gating

Based on the Cyber-Physical System design by Cinar et al. (2022): Continuous deep learning burns edge batteries; statistical gating saves >90% of edge CPU cycles.

STAGE 1: COARSE GATING sub-ms by design (not benchmarked)
Shewhart Statistical Process Control (SPC)

Tracks the rolling baseline mean ($\mu$) and variance ($\sigma$) of the machine. As long as the vibration envelope satisfies:

z = |x - μ| / σ ≤ 3.0

The machine is healthy. Heavy machine learning is kept asleep, saving power and thermal head.

STAGE 2: FINE DIAGNOSTICS 30.0 ms on Pi; 26.2 ms host
Ensemble Classifier (Random Forest / GBDT)

The moment Stage 1 detects a $3\sigma$ violation or Kurtosis shock, Stage 2 wakes up immediately to classify the exact failure fingerprint:

• Healthy (Class 0): Normal operational baseline.
• Mechanical Imbalance (Class 1): $1\times$ RPM sinusoidal wave.
• Bearing Defect (Class 2): High Kurtosis micro-impact shocks.

Physics & Waveform Kinematics

10 Mathematical Features & Diagnostic Physics

Why our model doesn't guess: It learns the mechanical kinematics formalized by Randall & Antoni (2011) and ISO 10816-3.

Kurtosis ($K$) — Shock Physics

Healthy vibration is Gaussian ($K \approx 3.0$). When a bearing ball strikes a microscopic pit, it produces microsecond impact spikes driving $K > 5.0$ to $15.0+$.

Crest Factor ($CF$)

$$CF = \frac{x_{\text{peak}}}{x_{\text{rms}}}$$ Ratio of peak amplitude to effective energy. Detects sharp impact spikes before overall RMS velocity climbs, serving as an early bearing wear indicator.

Engineering Robustness

Offline-First Resiliency: What If Internet Dies?

Industrial equipment cannot rely on fragile Wi-Fi. Our Cyber-Physical System continues operating even with Ethernet cables severed.

1. Local Ring Buffer
Every telemetry frame is written first to a persistent local circular buffer on the Pi's internal storage. If cloud connectivity drops, data is never lost.
2. Standalone Hotspot
Technicians in the plant can connect directly to the Raspberry Pi's local web server (http://192.168.4.1:5000) inside the factory without internet access.
3. Auto-Replay Sync
The moment factory Wi-Fi is restored, our asynchronous daemon (pdm-cloud) flushes the queue, back-filling Firebase with 100% data integrity.

100% Score Defense Key

Judge Defense Master Key: The 4 Toughest Questions

Memorize these 4 definitive answers to instantly dismantle common judge critiques and score maximum evaluation points.

Q1: Why didn't you just use an Arduino?

"First, the Capstone challenge strictly forbids Arduino. Second, an 8-bit Arduino AVR lacks a hardware floating-point unit (FPU) and cannot compute 100 Hz sliding window statistical moments, FFT spectral energies, or run 300-tree Random Forest models. Our Raspberry Pi 4 (Quad-Core 64-bit ARM) provides true real-time edge computing."

Q2: Why not stream all raw vibration directly to the Cloud?

"Streaming raw 100 Hz 3-axis vibration generates millions of records daily, creating heavy network latency, costly cloud egress fees, and catastrophic vulnerabilities if the factory internet cuts. We compute features locally in 1.8 ms and only stream lightweight status frames."

Q3: Why Random Forest over Deep Neural Networks?

"A 1D-CNN consumes ~65 MB RAM and takes 28.5 ms per inference, risking thermal throttling on embedded chips. Our Random Forest scores 100% on the physics-simulated benchmark at 30.0 ms per inference on the Raspberry Pi 4 (measured), with a small memory footprint and explainable tree split thresholds."

Q4: How did you prove your sensors are accurate?

"We performed a 6-position static gravitational calibration on an optical surface to zero the ADXL345 0g offset vector, and calibrated the thermocouple against crushed ice ($0.0^\circ\text{C}$) and boiling water ($100.0^\circ\text{C}$), achieving $< 0.51\%$ residual error."
